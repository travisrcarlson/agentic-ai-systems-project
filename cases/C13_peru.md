# Case file C13: A recorded minefield and a landslide below it in the Cordillera del Cóndor

> Invented case for an academic exercise. The country context comes from the public sources listed in the repository's README. The area, its ID, the people (named by role only), the survey and the national criteria box are invented, and do not describe a real hazardous area or a real national standard.

## 2. How the area was recorded
The army's minefield record for the post gives the outline of the minefield and the pattern of its rows. In 2021 a demining team cleared the minefield of a neighbouring post using a record made the same way, by the same unit, and found the mines where the record placed them. In 2025, after a landslide below the post, a demining team working nearby found a mine on the landslide scar, outside the recorded minefield, and the slope was added as an SHA. Both countries' army records for this stretch of the border were compared through the binational demining unit in 2016.

## 4. Community meeting
Held at the nearest community, a day's walk downstream, on 3 June 2026. The men (9) and the women (8) met separately; the female community liaison officer led the women's meeting. The retired army engineer was interviewed alone. People use the river below the slope but keep away from the slope and the post.

## 6. Survey team remarks
The record is reliable: the neighbouring post's minefield was found exactly where its record placed it. The mine on the landslide scar was a single item, moved by the landslide and already destroyed, so it was an EOD spot task. The team recommends clearance of the recorded minefield and cancelling the slope.
