# Case file C12: A slope below a former position in the Civilian Control Zone

> Invented case for an academic exercise. The country context comes from the public sources listed in the repository's README. The area, its ID, the people (named by role only), the survey and the national criteria box are invented, and do not describe a real hazardous area or a real national standard.

## 2. How the area was recorded
The military lists the slope as an "unconfirmed minefield": the position above it was manned from the 1970s to the 1990s, and its perimeter may have been mined, but no emplacement record is held. When the list was published in 2019, the county drew the suspected area to the edges of the land parcel, which takes in the gully and the paddy fields. The military had fenced the slope with warning signs as a precaution in 1998. No finds, accidents or clearance are recorded in the area.

## 4. Community meeting
Held at the village hall outside the Civilian Control Line on 7 October 2026. The men (8) and the women (7) met separately; the female liaison officer led the women's meeting. The former soldier attended the men's meeting and was then interviewed alone. Everyone keeps out of the fenced slope and the gully. The paddy fields have been farmed since the 1990s.

## 6. Survey team remarks
No mine has ever been found here, and nobody knows whether the perimeter was mined. The slope and the gully are protected habitat, and survey or clearance would damage them. The county asks for the whole area to be released for farming. The team recommends cancelling the whole area.
