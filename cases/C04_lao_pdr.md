# Case file C04: Upland village plots in a bombed valley

> Invented case for an academic exercise. The country context comes from the public sources listed in the repository's README. The area, its ID, the people (named by role only), the survey and the national criteria box are invented, and do not describe a real hazardous area or a real national standard.

## 2. How the area was recorded
The 2019 village survey recorded the area because of the valley's bombing history and the items that farmers find. In 2026 the team's desk assessment compared the EOD spot tasks, accident records and bombing data held in the national database with what the village reported.

## 4. Community meeting
Held at the village hall on 15 July 2026. The men (16) and the women (12) met separately, and a teacher brought a group of boys and girls, who said where they walk to school and to the stream. The farmers who found items drew the community map with the team. The village chief keeps a list of the finds he has reported.

## 5. Land use
Years of safe use count the whole years of use with no finds and no accidents.

| Part | Use | Since | Years of safe use |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Rice and vegetables, hand hoeing; items found during use | Before 1980 | 0 |
| B | Upland rice and maize on the slopes; items found during use | Before 1980 | 0 |

## 6. Survey team remarks
Submunitions have been found in both parts. The team recommends cluster munition remnant survey, starting from the evidence points, to define the confirmed hazardous areas before any clearance. The village chief asked the team what farmers should do when they find items while hoeing, and whether they may move them off their plots themselves so that they can keep working.
